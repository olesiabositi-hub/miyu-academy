# 1. First: what counts as a “monster” anyway?

There is no single neat category.

# 3. Are there definitely three heads?

Here we remember the rule from Module 1 again:

**myths have different versions.**

In Hesiod’s famous *Theogony*, Cerberus is described as a dog with **fifty heads**.

Later, the three-headed version became much more popular.

And honestly, humanity made the right choice.

Three heads still fit nicely into an image.

With fifty, the logo designer would simply quit.

So for our course:

### Cerberus → three-headed dog → guards the Underworld.

That is enough.

# 4. Why does IT love the name Cerberus?

Because the image hardly needs explanation.

There is a boundary.

There is someone guarding it.

There are people who should not get through.

**Guardian → access → security**

For example, there is a real product today called:

# Cerberus FTP Server

It is used for secure file transfer and supports security methods such as TLS, SFTP, HTTPS and two-factor authentication.

It is very tempting to say:

> “They called it Cerberus because Cerberus guarded the entrance to Hades.”

Logical?

Very.

Confirmed by the creators?

We do not have that confirmation.

So we apply our rule again:

✓ Cerberus is a real product;

✓ mythological Cerberus is a guard;

? why the developers chose this exact name — we do not invent it.

But now it is easy to understand why the image works so well for security.

# 11. What did sirens do?

Here, the familiar part stays the same.

# 12. Now look at your coffee cup

# Starbucks.

Yes.

**a two-tailed siren.**

Very confident coffee.

## Sources for fact-checking — editorial version only

Ancient descriptions give Cerberus different numbers of heads: Hesiod gives him fifty, while later tradition fixes the familiar three-headed image. His main role is to guard Hades’ realm and prevent the dead from leaving.

There were three Gorgons — Stheno, Euryale and Medusa; Medusa was the mortal one. The story of Perseus and Medusa’s petrifying gaze is well attested in ancient sources. Versace officially uses Medusa as one of the house’s key symbols.

In ancient tradition, the Minotaur is connected with Crete, King Minos, the Labyrinth and Theseus; the name Minotauros is literally connected with the “bull of Minos”.

Ancient Greek art usually shows sirens as beings with a woman’s head or upper body and the body of a bird. The story of their deadly song is especially famous from the *Odyssey*. Starbucks officially confirms that it uses the image of a two-tailed siren and connects it with the brand’s maritime history and the metaphor of the siren song.

The Lernaean Hydra is famous for growing new heads after others are cut off and is connected with a labour of Heracles. Ory Hydra is a real OAuth 2.0 / OpenID Connect server; the official page used here does not explain why the name was chosen.

Polyphemus is a one-eyed Cyclops, son of Poseidon, whom Odysseus blinds on his way home from Troy. This connection becomes central in the *Odyssey* module.
