# 1. And this is where we got the word “odyssey”

Today, an **odyssey** can mean:

# a long journey with many adventures and challenges.

It does not even have to be a real journey.

For example:

> “Moving to another country turned into a real odyssey.”

Or:

> “Getting this document was some kind of odyssey.”

The word comes directly from:

# the *Odyssey*

and its hero:

# Odysseus.

So one person’s name became a word for any journey that turned out to be:

> much more difficult than planned.

And that describes his road home perfectly.

# Mentor

# the name of a character in the *Odyssey*.

Odysseus leaves his old friend Mentor to look after his home and Telemachus.

Merriam-Webster traces the modern word *mentor* to the character in the *Odyssey* and to the later literary tradition around Telemachus.

## Sources for fact-checking — editorial version only

Polyphemus: in Book IX of the *Odyssey*, Odysseus calls himself “Nobody”, blinds Polyphemus, escapes from the cave under the sheep, and then, after sailing away, reveals his real name. Polyphemus then calls on his father Poseidon and asks him to make Odysseus’s return long and difficult.

Circe: in Book X of the *Odyssey*, she turns Odysseus’s companions into pigs; Hermes gives Odysseus the plant moly, which protects him from her magic. After their confrontation, Circe returns the men to human form; the crew stays with her for about a year, and then she helps Odysseus continue his journey.

The Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis: Book XII describes Circe’s instructions, the wax in the crew’s ears, Odysseus tied to the mast, and then the passage between Scylla and Charybdis. Scylla takes six companions; Circe directly explains that this risk is smaller than losing the whole ship to Charybdis.

The sacred cattle of Helios: Odysseus and his crew are warned not to touch the animals. After the companions kill them anyway, Zeus destroys the ship with a lightning bolt, and Odysseus is the only survivor.

Calypso: in Homer, Odysseus spends seven years with her. She offers him the chance to stay, including the promise of immortality, but he continues to want to return home to Penelope. After the gods decide that he must be allowed to leave, Calypso helps him leave the island.

Penelope and the return: ancient tradition describes her trick with the funeral shroud — she weaves it by day and undoes it at night for three years. After Odysseus returns, he acts in the form of a beggar; the test with his bow becomes part of revealing his identity and taking back control of his home.

The modern word **odyssey** comes from the title of Homer’s poem and is used for a long journey full of events and challenges.

The modern word **mentor** comes from the name Mentor in the *Odyssey*, a friend of Odysseus connected with Telemachus. Later, the seventeenth-century French literary tradition played an important role in turning the name into a common noun.
