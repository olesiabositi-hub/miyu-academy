# Module 2

# Who Is Who Among the Gods

**Time:** about 20–25 minutes

In the previous module, we finally understood where Zeus, Poseidon and Hades came from.

Very simply:

**Cronus + Rhea → the next generation of gods**

And now we can move on to the group people usually come to Greek mythology for.

The gods.

But let’s agree on one thing right away: we are **not going to memorise a table of twelve names**.

I hate tables like that too.

Five minutes later, what stays in your head is something like:

> Apollo — something about the Sun?  
> And Artemis is something about the Moon too?  
> Athena and Ares are both about war?  
> So who is Hermes then?

So we will meet the gods as characters.

For each one, we only need to understand:

**who they are → how they are different from the others → what is easy to connect them with in your mind.**

---

# 1. First: who are the Olympians?

The name is quite literal.

**The Olympian gods are the main gods connected with Mount Olympus.**

And yes, Olympus is not an imaginary place.

It is a real mountain in Greece, but in mythology its peaks become the home of the gods.

There is just one small trap here.

Do not think of “Olympians” as a separate biological species.

It is more like **the ruling group of gods from the new generation**.

And not every relative of Zeus necessarily lives on Olympus.

We already know the best example:

### Hades

He is the brother of Zeus and Poseidon.

He belongs to the same generation.

But he lives and rules in the Underworld.

So he is usually not included in the usual list of the Twelve Olympians.

Another reason I do not want to make you memorise the “correct twelve”: in ancient tradition, the list of the main gods was not always exactly the same.

It is more important for us to understand the characters than to pass a test on a list.

---

# 2. Zeus — the one at the top

We already know Zeus.

By lot, he received:

# the Sky

So we get:

thunder,  
lightning,  
power over the sky.

But over time, Zeus becomes more than just “the god of the sky”.

He becomes:

# the main ruler of the gods.

Very roughly:

**Zeus = power**

And here is one tip.

Do not try to remember every Zeus story at once.

Because if we open the full list of his relationships, children and transformations…

we will never leave this module.

For now, this is enough:

**Zeus → sky → thunder → power.**

---

# 3. Hera — not just “Zeus’s jealous wife”

Hera is Zeus’s sister.

And also his wife.

Yes, family relationships among the Greek gods become the kind of thing you just have to accept quite quickly.

Hera is:

# the goddess of marriage and queen of the gods.

And modern pop culture has damaged her reputation a little.

In many retellings, Hera looks almost only like a woman who runs after Zeus’s next lover and takes revenge on everyone.

There really are many stories like that.

But if we keep only those stories, we lose the main point.

For the ancient Greeks, Hera was connected with:

**marriage, lawful union, women’s status and royal power.**

So for now:

### Simple association

**Hera → marriage + queen**

We will need her again when Heracles appears.

And then it will become clear why the name **Heracles** literally contains Hera’s name — even though their relationship is, to put it gently, complicated.

But later.

# 4. Poseidon — the sea, but not only the sea

Poseidon received the sea by lot.

So the simplest connection is:

# Poseidon → sea

His main recognisable symbol is:

# the trident.

If you see a bearded god with a trident, it is probably Poseidon.

But here is an unexpected detail.

He is also connected with:

**earthquakes**

and

**horses**.

Yes, sea god + horses sounds like a fairly random combination.

But these strange connections are what make the Greek gods feel like living characters instead of job descriptions.

You do not need to remember them yet.

The main thing is:

**Poseidon → sea → trident.**

---

# 5. We already know Hades

I am deliberately not going to tell his whole story again.

Let’s only check whether the main idea stayed with you:

# Hades → Underworld

Not:

**Hades → evil.**

This really matters, because later we will meet his kingdom, his wife Persephone and his famous guard.

And the habit of thinking “Hades = Devil” will only get in the way.

---

# 6. Athena — think first, fight second

Here is one of my favourite characters.

# Athena

She is usually called the goddess of:

**wisdom, strategy, crafts and war.**

And here you may have a logical question:

> Wait.  
> Isn’t Ares the god of war?

Yes.

And that is exactly why it is useful to learn them together.

---

# 7. Athena vs Ares

Both are connected with war.

But they represent **completely different sides of it**.

### Athena

Strategy.

Planning.

Calculation.

Discipline.

Winning with your brain.

### Ares

Battle rage.

Aggression.

Physical force.

Brutality.

Conflict itself.

If we translate this almost into the language of a work meeting:

**Athena will open the board first, look at the data and make a plan.**

**Ares has already burst into the meeting room.**

Of course, this is our modern simplification.

But it helps you remember the difference very well.

### Athena → strategy

### Ares → rage of war

# 8. Why is Athens connected with Athena?

There is a beautiful story here that is really useful to know.

In the myth, Athena and Poseidon compete to become the patron of the city.

Each one has to give the people a gift.

Poseidon creates a water spring — and in some later versions there is also a connection with a horse.

Athena gives:

# an olive tree.

Her gift is judged more useful.

And Athena becomes the patron of the city.

So the connection:

**Athena ↔ Athens**

is not random inside Greek mythological tradition.

And next time you see the Parthenon in photos of Athens, you will already understand why the main temple on the Acropolis is dedicated to Athena.

---

# 9. Athena in IT

If you work with data, the name **Athena** may also be familiar.

AWS has a service called:

# Amazon Athena

It is a real serverless query service for data analysis.

But here we introduce an important rule of our course.

We know:

✓ the product is really called Athena;

✓ Athena is the name of an ancient Greek goddess.

# 10. Aphrodite — and Cyprus appears

# Aphrodite

The simplest association is:

**love, beauty, desire, attraction.**

But Aphrodite is a little more interesting because there are several versions of her origin.

In one tradition, she is the daughter of Zeus and Dione.

In Hesiod’s famous version, Aphrodite appears from:

# sea foam.

And this version gave us one of the most recognisable images in ancient mythology:

Aphrodite rising from the sea.

---

# 11. What does Cyprus have to do with it?

A lot.

Aphrodite’s connection with Cyprus is ancient and very strong.

Ancient texts even call her:

**Cypria — “the Cyprian”.**

One of the Homeric Hymns speaks about sea-girt Cyprus as Aphrodite’s land.

So if you live in Cyprus, mythology suddenly becomes much less abstract.

---

# 12. If you live in Limassol

About **11 kilometres east of central Limassol** there is the ancient site of:

# Amathous

Today it is an archaeological site.

In the ancient world, it was one of the important city-kingdoms of Cyprus.

And there was a sanctuary of:

# Aphrodite.

The official tourism resource of Cyprus describes Amathous as an important centre of the cult of **Aphrodite-Astarte**; among the archaeological remains are the ruins of Aphrodite’s temple.

So if you live in Limassol, there is a very real chance that you have passed many times very close to a place where people worshipped Aphrodite thousands of years ago.

These are the moments I love most in mythology.

At some point it stops being:

> “some ancient Greeks and some gods”.

And turns out to be literally nearby.

### Simple association:

**Aphrodite → love and attraction → Cyprus**

# 13. Apollo — music, prophecy and NASA

# Apollo

Here again, I do not want to reduce a character to one job.

He is connected with several important areas:

**music**

**poetry**

**prophecy**

**archery**

and later develops a strong connection with solar imagery.

But if you keep just two things:

### Apollo → art + prophecy

that is already enough.

The city of Delphi and the famous Oracle of Apollo at Delphi are especially important.

We may return to this later.

But right now, we are interested in a very different object.

---

# 14. Apollo 11 was not called Apollo by accident

NASA really named its programme:

# Apollo

after the ancient Greek god.

The name was suggested in 1960 by NASA manager Abe Silverstein. NASA’s official history says that the name was chosen because of the attractive mythological associations.

So:

**Apollo → NASA Apollo**

is not our pretty guess.

It is a documented fact.

And when Apollo 11 later took humans to the Moon, an ancient name became part of very modern history.

---

# 15. And now — his sister

Apollo has a twin sister:

# Artemis

She is connected with:

**hunting**

**wild nature**

**the bow**

and the protection of girls and young women.

Later, Artemis also developed a strong connection with the Moon.

### Simple association:

**Artemis → wild nature + hunting**

And now it becomes especially interesting.

---

# 16. NASA continued the story

NASA’s modern programme to return people to the Moon is called:

# Artemis

And here the connection is completely intentional.

NASA directly explains:

**Artemis is Apollo’s twin sister.**

So after the Apollo programme, the new lunar programme received the name Artemis.

This is a perfect modern reference because the name helps you remember the myth itself:

**Apollo ↔ Artemis**

**Apollo ↔ Artemis**

**twin brother and sister.**

# 17. Hermes — the one who is always going somewhere

# Hermes

If you need a god who rarely sits still, this is him.

Hermes is:

**the messenger of the gods.**

He is connected with:

travel,  
roads,  
trade,  
boundaries,  
the delivery of messages.

In images, he is easy to recognise by:

**winged sandals**

or a winged hat

and a staff.

### Simple association:

**Hermes → movement + messages**

---

# 18. Developers may already have met Hermes

Meta has a JavaScript engine called:

# Hermes

created for React Native.

It is very tempting to say immediately:

> “Of course! Hermes was the messenger, so that is why they called the engine Hermes!”

But…

stop.

We do not actually know that for sure.

Meta confirms the product name and describes the engine, but the official material we use does not explain a mythological reason for choosing the name.

So we will not invent a beautiful explanation.

We can only say:

> You may already have seen the ancient Greek name Hermes directly in your development stack.

And that is enough.

---

# 19. Is Hermès also named after the god?

This is a perfect example of why sources matter.

The French house:

# Hermès

looks like an obvious mythological reference.

Hermes — god of travel.

Hermès — luxury brand.

It fits nicely.

Except this is **not that case**.

The house Hermès is named after its founder:

# Thierry Hermès

who opened his workshop in Paris in 1837.

So:

**Hermes JavaScript engine → a modern product with a mythological name**

but

**Hermès fashion house → the founder’s surname.**

A very useful trap.

Not everything that sounds ancient Greek was really named after mythology.

---

# 20. And finally — Nike

Almost everyone knows this goddess.

Many people just do not know that they know her.

# Nike

She is not one of the main Olympian goddesses.

Nike is:

# the personification of victory.

So here it is almost literal:

**Nike = Victory**

And now guess where the sports brand got its name.

Yes.

This time, it really is that simple.

Nike itself confirms that the company took its name from:

# the Greek goddess of victory — Nike.

And this may be one of the best modern mythological references ever.

Sport.

Competition.

Victory.

# Nike.

Once you know where the name comes from, it starts to feel almost too obvious.

## Sources for fact-checking — do not publish inside the lesson

The main spheres and roles of the Olympian gods were checked against ancient hymns and the Theoi reference corpus. The Homeric Hymns describe Athena as connected with war and crafts, Artemis with hunting, Aphrodite with love and desire; Theoi also collects ancient evidence about the spheres of Zeus, Poseidon, Hermes and other gods.

The story of Athena and Poseidon competing for Athens and Athena’s gift of the olive tree is based on ancient tradition represented by Apollodorus, Pausanias and other authors.

Aphrodite’s sea birth and connection with Cyprus are attested in Hesiod and the Homeric Hymns. Archaeological Amathous in the Limassol region is officially described as an important centre of the cult of Aphrodite-Astarte.

NASA documents the mythological origin of the name Apollo, and directly explains the name Artemis through Apollo’s twin sister.

Nike officially states that the company name comes from the Greek goddess of victory. Meta confirms the existence of the Hermes JavaScript engine, but the official material used here does not explain why that name was chosen. The house of Hermès, by contrast, documents that its name comes from founder Thierry Hermès.
