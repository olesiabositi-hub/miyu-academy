# 8. Very important: the *Iliad* is NOT the whole Trojan War

This is worth remembering.

People often think:

> “The *Iliad* = the story of the Trojan War from beginning to end.”

No.

Homer enters the story when the war:

The *Iliad* has:

❌ no beginning of the war;

❌ no famous death of Achilles;

❌ no Trojan Horse;

❌ no fall of Troy.

So the most famous object of the Trojan War —

# the Trojan Horse —

is not actually a scene in the *Iliad* at all.

## Sources for fact-checking — editorial version only

The Judgement of Paris: in ancient tradition, Eris starts the argument between Hera, Athena and Aphrodite, and Paris chooses Aphrodite after she promises him Helen. Details of the golden apple and the goddesses’ offers appear in several ancient versions.

Helen and Paris: ancient sources describe Helen’s role and Aphrodite’s involvement differently, so the lesson deliberately does not fix one single version as either “abduction” or “voluntary escape”.

The line Achilles → refusal to fight → Patroclus in his armour → Patroclus killed by Hector → Achilles returns → Hector dies is attested in the *Iliad* and later mythological summaries. The final book of the *Iliad* ends with the return of Hector’s body to Priam and his burial, not with the fall of Troy.

The wooden horse is not the final episode of the *Iliad*. In the surviving summary of the Epic Cycle, Epeius builds the horse under Athena’s guidance, the best Greek warriors hide inside, and the Trojans drag it into the city. Odysseus plays a key role in the operation.

NIST defines a Trojan horse as a program that appears useful or harmless but contains a hidden harmful function, and directly connects the term with the wooden horse from Greek mythology.

Apache Cassandra is a real distributed NoSQL database. The official Apache materials used here confirm the system’s purpose but do not explain the mythological origin of the name, so the lesson does not claim a naming motive.
