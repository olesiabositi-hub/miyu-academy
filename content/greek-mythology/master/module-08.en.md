# 5. Three levels of confidence

When we meet a mythological name in the modern world, we ask:

# what do we actually know?

### Level 1 — confirmed

The creator explains the origin directly.

For example:

**Nike → goddess of victory.**

Confirmed by Nike.

---

### Level 2 — a modern object really does have a mythological name

For example:

# Amazon Athena.

This service really exists.

It is an AWS serverless service for analysing data with SQL.

But if AWS does not say:

> “We chose the name Athena specifically because Athena is the goddess of wisdom”,

we do not write that sentence for AWS.

---

### Level 3 — our interpretation

We can say:

> “I can see why the name Cerberus sounds good for security.”

Because:

**Cerberus → guards the entrance.**

But until the creators themselves confirm that logic:

# it is our interpretation.

And that is fine.

The important thing is:

do not present it as a fact.

# The Myth Decoder rule

🟢 **Confirmed by the creator**

🟡 **The name matches**

⚪ **A meaningful connection**

> “This image fits the meaning well.”

But you cannot say:

> “That is why they named it this way.”

## Sources for fact-checking — editorial version only

Nike officially states that the company name comes from Nike, the Greek goddess of victory.

Starbucks officially describes its logo as a two-tailed Siren from Greek mythology and connects the choice of a maritime image with Seattle and the seafaring history of coffee.

Versace continues to officially use Medusa as one of its recognisable House codes.

AWS confirms Amazon Athena as a serverless interactive query service; Meta confirms Hermes as a JavaScript engine for React Native; Prometheus is an open-source monitoring and alerting toolkit; Apache describes Cassandra as a distributed NoSQL database. The official materials used here do not provide enough evidence to assign these companies a specific mythological reason for choosing the names.

NIST defines a Trojan horse as a program with an apparently useful function and a hidden potentially harmful function; the technical metaphor matches the logic of the mythological Trojan Horse.

NASA directly explains the modern Apollo/Artemis pair through the mythological Apollo and his twin sister Artemis.

Cyprus’s official tourism resource confirms that Amathous is about 11 km east of central Limassol, was an important place of worship of Aphrodite-Astarte, and is connected with a local tradition about Theseus and Ariadne. The Sanctuary of Apollo Hylates is about 22 km west of Limassol and was an important religious centre of ancient Kourion.

Batumi’s official tourism resource confirms that Europe Square has a statue of Medea holding the Golden Fleece, referring to Colchis and the myth of the Argonauts.

NASA describes Orion as one of the most recognisable constellations and connects its modern name with the Greek hunter Orion. The international astronomical system includes 88 official constellations; Greek mythology is only one of the cultural traditions used to interpret the sky.
